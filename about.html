About Robo Kyle

A small studio with one stubborn belief.

The web was built to be open. Somewhere along the way it stopped being open to everyone. We’re a nonprofit trying to nudge that back — one accessible tool, one honest guide, one paid co-design session at a time.

Why we exist

More than a billion people live with a disability. Most of the software they touch every day wasn’t designed with them in mind. It works — barely — when it works at all, and the workarounds fall on the person, not the product.

Robo Kyle exists to shift that weight. We build small, focused tools that solve real problems for real people, and we publish everything we learn so other teams can do the same.

How we work

We co-design. That means the first sketch is drawn with a disabled tester in the room, not after the fact. It means we pay for that time — access labour is labour. And it means we’re willing to throw away a beautiful prototype the moment it stops serving the person it was built for.

Everything we ship is free, open source, and documented in plain language. If a tool we made ends up buried inside somebody else’s product with our name filed off, we count that as a win.

What we value

  • Access before polish A rough tool that works with a screen reader beats a beautiful one that doesn’t.
  • Pay for lived experience Testers, advisors, and co-designers get invoiced hours, not gift cards.
  • Plain language, always If we can’t explain it to a grade-6 reader, we don’t understand it yet.
  • Open by default Code, guides, decisions, budgets. All in the open, all forkable.

The story so far

  1. 2024 The studio starts. A handful of engineers, designers, and disabled advisors decide the accessibility gap is worth a nonprofit, not a side project.
  2. 2025 First tools ship. A caption remixer, a colour-contrast checker, and a keyboard-first file picker land in production — all under permissive licenses.
  3. 2026 Guides go public. We open up the plain-language guide library so other teams can build accessible work without reinventing the checklist.
  4. Next Grow the co-design bench. Expanding the paid tester network so every project has three voices in the room from day one.

Want to help?

We’re a small team and there’s more to do than we can carry alone. If you build software, test it with lived experience, teach accessibility, or just want to point us at a broken tool that’s hurting people, we’d love to hear from you.

Design

One-Handed Keyboard Rest

A printable wedge that sits under one side of a standard keyboard, tilting it toward the typing hand and raising the wrist to a neutral angle. Two versions: left-dominant and right-dominant. Fits every full-size keyboard we’ve tested and most tenkeyless boards too.

It helps people typing with one hand — after stroke, amputation, hemiplegia, or a temporary injury — work longer without fatigue or wrist strain.

How it works and why we designed it this way

A one-handed typist reaches across the keyboard constantly. Angling the board 12° toward the typing hand shortens the average reach and lets the wrist stay in a neutral, unrotated position. The wedge height also raises the far edge of the keyboard slightly, which naturally biases the eye toward the letters closest to the typing hand.

Why not a split keyboard?

Split keyboards are excellent for two-handed typing. For a one-handed typist they double the reach on the non-typing half, which is exactly the wrong direction. A wedge under a stock board keeps the layout the person already knows.

What we got wrong, and what we would change

  • V1 was too tall for people with shorter forearms. V2 offers a 12° and an 8° variant.
  • The underside was flat; on glass desks it slid around. V2 has recessed pockets for adhesive silicone feet.

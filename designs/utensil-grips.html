Design

Adaptive Utensil Grip Set

A single-print set of three slip-on grips that add a fatter, textured handle to standard household utensils — forks, spoons, toothbrushes, hairbrushes, razors. Slides on with warm water, stays put in the dishwasher.

It helps people with reduced grip strength, tremor, arthritis, or hand hypermobility hold everyday tools without cramping.

In progress 3D printer

What’s honest about the status

Two of the three sizes fit reliably. The largest size (for wider hairbrush handles) is still being tuned — the current print is loose on some handles and cracks on others. We’re re-testing with TPU rather than PETG.

  • PartsOne printed grip set (three sizes on one plate).
  • Tools3D printer capable of TPU (soft filament). Warm water for slip-on fit.
  • Rough costUnder $3 in filament for all three.
  • Build time~3 hours print, no assembly.

How it works and why we designed it this way

Each grip is a hollow textured cylinder with an inner taper. The taper squeezes the utensil handle as it slides in, so a single design fits a range of handle diameters within its size class. The exterior is a subtle knurl pattern — enough traction to help, not sharp enough to be uncomfortable during long use.

Why TPU and not silicone?

Silicone is warmer to the touch and more forgiving, but requires casting. TPU can be printed at home with the same equipment people already use for the rest of our designs. The tradeoff is a slightly firmer feel.

What we got wrong, and what we would change

  • Our first grips were solid PETG. They were too rigid and cracked under torque.
  • We underestimated how much handle sizes vary between brands. The three-size approach came from measuring 40 utensils, not our first-guess two sizes.
  • The current large size has a wall thickness that’s marginal for TPU. Fix in progress.

Design

Voice-Activated Lamp Base

A hollow, printed lamp base that houses an ESP32-S3 board running a small on-device wake-word model. Swap it in under any standard lamp and you can turn the light on and off, dim it, or set brightness presets by voice — without a single packet ever leaving the room.

It’s built for anyone who wants voice control without an Alexa or Google Home in the house: people with sensitive access needs, disabled users who’ve had bad experiences with cloud assistants misrouting their voices, or anyone tired of paying subscription fees to turn a light on.

Experimental Electronics

What’s honest about the status

Two prototypes work in quiet rooms. The wake-word model needs retraining for accented English — it currently fails often for two of our four testers. Downloadable files are held back until that gap closes. Docs are here so you can follow the work.

How it works and why we designed it this way

A small microphone on the base always listens for the wake word, running inference locally on the ESP32-S3. When it fires, a short command grammar (about 12 phrases) is matched against the next second of audio — also on-device. No audio, no transcript, no state ever leaves the lamp.

A matched command drives a TRIAC dimmer that sits between the mains cord and the bulb socket. It supports any dimmable incandescent, halogen, or dimmable LED bulb up to 200 W.

Why offline-only?

The people we co-designed this with had all been burned by cloud assistants — hot-mic incidents, misrouted commands, subscription creep. Offline was a hard requirement from session one, and it kept the design honest.

What we got wrong, and what we would change

  • Our wake-word training set was too narrow. We’re rebuilding it with volunteer voice donations across a wider accent range.
  • V1 mounted the microphone flush with the base — heat from the bulb warmed it and drifted the noise floor. V2 moves it to a cutout at the bottom rim.
  • We shipped without a tactile power-off. Testers unanimously wanted a physical override for the times voice control just doesn’t recognise them.

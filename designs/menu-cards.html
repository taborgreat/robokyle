Design

Large-Print Menu Cards

A set of printable, high-contrast menu templates — 18pt, 24pt, and 36pt type, black on cream (or white on black), with generous line spacing and no reversed-out italics. Fill in your own items in the editable file or hand-write them in the blank version.

Useful for restaurants and cafes that want to hand a large-print menu to any guest who asks, and for families making holiday dinners readable for older or low-vision relatives.

How it works and why we designed it this way

Restaurant menus often optimise for atmosphere over legibility — thin serifs, low-contrast greys, tight leading. The result is that a big share of guests can’t read the menu comfortably, and asking for help is embarrassing enough that many just order what they know.

These templates flip the defaults: heavy sans-serif type, 4.5:1 minimum contrast, line-height at 1.5, no all-caps runs longer than a heading. Cream paper reduces glare compared to bright white. Every design choice is a WCAG-inspired default applied to paper.

Why three sizes?

18pt covers most low-vision readers. 24pt is the comfortable choice for older readers without a diagnosis. 36pt handles severe visual impairment and reads well from a small distance for people who prefer to arm-length their reading.

What we got wrong, and what we would change

  • V1 shipped Word docs only. Half the users we spoke to didn’t have Word. V2 adds PDF and editable ODT.
  • The 18pt version was originally 16pt. Testers pushed us up. Always trust the testers.
  • We’d add a landscape-orientation variant next — some cafes use trifold table cards.

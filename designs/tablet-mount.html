Design

Bedside Tablet Mount

A printable clamp-and-cradle system that mounts on a bed frame, desk, or wheelchair armrest and holds a tablet at any angle without needing to be held. Uses a cheap flexible steel gooseneck as the arm.

Designed for people who read, video-call, or watch in bed for long stretches — especially anyone with limited arm strength or chronic pain that makes holding a tablet impractical.

Working 3D printer

What’s honest about the status

Design has held tablets up to 12.9" (iPad Pro) reliably. Testers have used it daily for over a year. We haven’t tested anything heavier than that.

  • PartsPrinted clamp, printed cradle, one 18" flexible steel gooseneck, 3 × M6 bolts and knobs.
  • Tools3D printer (PETG recommended for the clamp), Allen key.
  • Rough cost$14–$18 including gooseneck and hardware.
  • Build time30 minutes assembly plus ~7 hours print time.

How it works and why we designed it this way

The clamp uses a screw-tension design that grips bed rails, desks up to 2" thick, and standard wheelchair armrests without any modification. A flexible steel gooseneck between clamp and cradle lets the tablet be posed in any orientation and holds that pose without drift.

The cradle uses a friction-fit corner design that adjusts to any tablet from 7" to 13" without needing separate prints per device.

Why a gooseneck instead of a hinge?

Hinges wear out fast and require two hands to reposition. A gooseneck can be pushed into place with one hand or an elbow, and holds against gravity without a locking screw.

What we got wrong, and what we would change

  • V1 used PLA for the clamp. It crept over months of tension. V2 is PETG only.
  • The cradle’s corner grips were sharp at the tip — scratched two tablet cases before we chamfered them.
  • We should have supplied a short cable-management channel on the arm. Users kept improvising with tape.

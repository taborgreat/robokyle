What we do

Four ways we chip away at the access gap.

Every project we ship falls into one of these buckets — and every one of them is free to use, remix, and improve.

Adaptive tools

Small, focused apps that solve one real problem well — a caption remixer, a keyboard-only file picker, a low-vision colour tester.

Plain-language guides

Step-by-step walkthroughs for builders who want to ship accessible work without wading through 300-page spec documents.

Co-design sessions

We pay disabled testers for their time, from first sketch to final release. If it doesn’t work for them, it doesn’t ship.

Open source, forever

Every tool ships under a permissive license. Fork it, rebrand it, embed it in your product — just keep it accessible.

Our approach

Access isn’t a feature. It’s the whole point.

Most software treats accessibility like a compliance checkbox — something bolted on at the end, if there’s time. We do it in reverse. We start with the constraint and let it shape the design.

  • Every prototype is tested with a screen reader on day one.
  • Keyboard, switch, and voice paths are first-class, not fallback.
  • Copy is written for a grade-6 reader and reviewed for jargon.
  • Colour, motion, and sound all have off-switches.
